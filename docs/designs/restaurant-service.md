## Decision and problem statement

Build a production-oriented restaurant service whose first milestone is a convincing, connected sales demo. The user requires ordering, management, marketing and voice together. First live fulfillment is dine-in and takeaway, with payment recorded at the restaurant. **Complete this workflow before adding online payment.** Restaurant-managed delivery and unified Zomato/Swiggy operations remain later stages, subject to integration feasibility.

The immediate business goal is winning a first paid restaurant project. Product usefulness is not yet validated: local restaurants are reachable, but no owner has requested this product, described a specific pain or offered to pay. The demo is a way to test the offer, not evidence that the offer will sell.

## Demand evidence and status quo

- User: “Nobody yet” when asked about conversations or demand evidence.
- User reports offline orders at the counter or through waiters, and online orders mostly through Zomato and Swiggy. No observation of staff workflow, error rate, cost or buyer willingness to switch is available.
- Fragmented order management, customer uncertainty and repeat-customer marketing are hypotheses. The user explicitly chose “haven't observed a specific problem yet.”
- Target for the first conversation: an accessible local restaurant owner. Restaurant name, existing billing system, devices, order volume and procurement constraints are unknown. Do not invent a customer profile or revenue forecast.

### Customer journey

Open a restaurant link or table QR code, choose dine-in or takeaway, and see menu categories/items first, with a visible “Speak your order” action alongside browsing (design D3=A). Browse/customize or speak an order, review the proposal, then explicitly submit the reviewed basket. The first live release shows “Pay at restaurant.” A durable submission starts as Submitted, not accepted by staff. Staff acknowledgement changes the displayed status. A declined order explains the reason; a lost response is resolved using the existing idempotent recovery path.

No orderable items (design D9=A): after a successful availability read, replace the ordering list with “No items are available to order right now,” restaurant identity, guidance to ask restaurant staff and Check again. Preserve navigation and access to existing basket/status. Do not infer closure, promise a reopening time or clear the basket. Apply D7 to stale basket items; voice cannot bypass availability. Loading and request failures remain separate states.

Wider customer menu (design D26=A): at a minimum 64rem viewport width, when the menu retains at least 22rem and the summary 18rem usable width, show the menu left and a read-only selected-item summary with View basket right. Reuse the same basket state and current item data, without a second total calculation or checkout. Empty summary uses D12; narrower/zoomed layouts use D22. Preserve state on resize and menu-then-summary reading order. Existing explicit review, submission and recovery remain unchanged.

Phone basket access (design D22=A): while browsing the menu with selected items, show a bottom-pinned “View basket · [item count]” action opening review, never submitting. Reserve space for the bar and device safe areas; do not obscure controls or keyboard focus. Use at least a 44px target. Fall back to document flow when enlarged text or constrained height makes pinning obstructive. Retain ordinary basket-link access when empty; D12 defines that screen.

Empty basket (design D12=A): show “Your basket is empty” with Browse menu returning to the same restaurant menu and preserving restaurant/fulfillment context. Checkout is unavailable and no fees are invented. D9 handles unavailable menus; D6 recovery takes precedence for unresolved submissions.

Unavailable basket items (design D7=A): before submission, retain affected rows with “No longer available” and Remove. Block submission until resolved, then obtain a fresh server quote for explicit review. Customers may browse the menu for another item; never silently remove or substitute. During uncertain checkout, preserve the submitted payload and follow D6; basket repair resumes only after a definitive non-commit result.

Checkout recovery (design D6=A): show a dedicated recovery view with “We’re checking whether your order was received,” the submitted summary and Check order status using the existing same-key/body recovery path. Replace submission while unresolved; reload restores recovery. Only a confirmed receipt leads to order status; a definitive non-commit/validation result returns to basket review with an explanation. An ongoing outage remains visibly uncertain, without inviting a new submission. This does not require a new endpoint or automatic polling.

Takeaway is immediate pickup only initially; scheduled pickup, split bills and shared-table tabs are outside the first release. Each guest submission is a separate order. A QR code identifies a restaurant/table, not permission to view other diners' orders. Dine-in table assignments must resolve to active tables at that restaurant; staff confirm questionable assignments before preparation. A table link is shareable and does not prove physical presence. Apply bounded order abuse controls and retain staff acceptance as the preparation gate.

No delivery address is required for this first release. Collect only contact information justified for pickup, with a separate optional marketing opt-in. Transactional order updates do not imply promotional consent. Anonymous guest cookies continue to scope customer reads; no public table-wide order list or cross-device recovery by guessed order number.

### Staff and owner management

Owner workspace navigation (design D4=A): persistent Overview, Orders, Menu, Marketing and Settings destinations, with a page title and current-section indicator. Overview contains activity/payment summaries; Orders contains operational/payment work; Menu contains catalog changes; Marketing contains campaigns; Settings contains restaurant details/hours/tables, staff access and messaging setup. D21=A places navigation beneath the owner header on phones, with wrapping text links, current-section indication and at least 44px tap targets. Keep it in document flow on every owner page. D25=A keeps this same navigation beneath the header at every width, in one row when it fits and wrapping otherwise; no wider-screen sidebar.

Authenticated owners invite/revoke staff. Proposed initial roles: owner (configuration, membership, marketing and reports), order staff (queue/status and availability), cashier (payment recording). One person can hold multiple roles. Server authorization binds every read and write to membership and restaurant, including exports and jobs; UI hiding is not enforcement. Choose a supported authentication provider during engineering planning; do not build credential management from scratch.

Queue: Submitted → Accepted → Preparing → Ready → Completed. Rejection is allowed before acceptance; subsequent cancellation requires an authorized action and recorded reason. Illegal, stale and repeated transitions do not corrupt state. Use version checks and idempotent actions. Keep operational status outside the immutable priced order snapshot, with actor/time history. Completed means served/collected, not paid.

Staff phone order details (design D23=A): use a concise queue list leading to a separate full-width detail view with Back to queue and restoration of prior queue position where possible. Show order reference, items/modifiers, operational status and separate role-authorized payment state/actions. Refresh current state before mutations; returning to a prior queue snapshot does not make it current. Preserve D5, D10 and D14 feedback/recovery rules.

Wider staff workspace (design D24=A): show queue left and selected detail right at a minimum 64rem viewport width only when both panes retain at least 22rem usable width; otherwise use D23 full-width drill-down. Reuse identical detail content/actions. An unselected detail region says “Select an order to view details.” Keep the selected order identifiable and stable through refresh/status changes, and preserve selection and pending action identity across layout changes. Validate sizing with zoom and actual devices.

Empty staff queue (design D11=A): after a successful read confirms no active orders, retain the heading/navigation and show “No active orders right now,” last-updated time and Refresh. Keep access to other order/payment records available; do not automatically show completed orders. This is not a claim that every order is paid. If a filtered view exists, distinguish zero matching results from an empty overall queue.

Staff connection loss (design D5=A): keep the last successfully loaded queue visible with a persistent “Connection lost — orders may be out of date” banner, last-updated time and Retry. Disable order/payment/availability changes until a successful current-state refresh. Before any successful load, show an unavailable state with Retry, never an empty queue. Reconcile previously uncertain mutations through the existing idempotent path before enabling conflicting actions.

Payment is a separate recorded state: Unpaid → Paid through cashier/owner action, with amount, currency, method, actor and time. Initially support full payment only. Record a correction/reversal as an audited event, never overwrite the prior event. Money movement and physical refunds occur at the restaurant; the application records them and must not claim to process them. Rejected/cancelled paid orders surface a required settlement task. Reports distinguish recorded payments, unpaid amounts, reversals and order counts. They are not a bank reconciliation or a certified accounting system.

Payment entry (design D10=A): use an inline form in order detail showing the order reference, full amount/currency and required payment-method selection. “Record payment received” is the single explicit confirmation. Show Saving while pending and prevent duplicate submission; show Paid only after server confirmation. Preserve entered details on definitive rejection and explain the error. Reconcile uncertain results with the same action identity before another payment attempt; D5 governs connection loss. No extra confirmation dialog or online money movement.

Owners manage branding, hours, tables, item/modifier prices and availability. Order staff may change availability only. Changes affect future quotes and orders, never historical snapshots. New submissions respect restaurant ordering hours and current availability. Start with simple activity and payment summaries, not inventory, payroll or accounting.

### Voice

Microphone or typed input produces a visible draft with restaurant-scoped item IDs, quantities and modifiers. Ask for clarification on ambiguity, unsupported requests or missing required choices. Every basket change requires confirmation and normal server validation. Voice never checks out, marks payment, changes staff permissions or sends a campaign autonomously.

Show listening/stopped/error states and provide manual ordering when permission is denied, speech is unsupported, the network fails or recognition is unclear. Declare the supported devices and language after real-device testing; multilingual support is not promised. Do not persist raw audio by default. Any external speech/model provider requires an explicit data-flow and cost decision before live rollout. Browser speech availability alone is not production coverage.

### Marketing service

Marketing first visit (design D13=A): show “No campaigns yet” with Create campaign draft as the primary action, verified messaging-setup status and a secondary Settings link. Unknown setup remains unverified. Drafting can precede setup; all existing onboarding, consent, recipient, cost and approval gates still block sending when unmet. Synthetic-demo external sends remain disabled.

First release includes owner-reviewed SMS and email campaigns; WhatsApp is not added implicitly. A campaign draft combines a defined offer, audience, channel and message. The owner reviews recipient count, consent eligibility, offer limits/expiry and estimated send cost, then approves that exact version. Editing invalidates approval. Audience data comes from restaurant-scoped, consented first-party contacts; do not assume access to delivery-platform customer lists.

An assistant may draft text, but deterministic rules own audience eligibility, discount validity, quotas and suppression. Recheck consent and unsubscribe state at send time. Use durable per-recipient delivery records, bounded retries and provider deduplication where available; ambiguous provider outcomes enter reconciliation, not blind resend. Isolate tenant credentials and validate provider callbacks. Capture send failure, provider acceptance, delivery and redemption separately. Do not infer revenue uplift from delivered messages.

Approval freezes the deduplicated recipient set, channel, message, offer version and maximum total send cost. Send-time checks may remove recipients, never add them. A changed audience, higher cost estimate or edited offer requires renewed owner approval. The worker stops before exceeding the approved cap; remaining work is visibly paused rather than silently increasing spend.

First-release discount redemption uses a restaurant-scoped public promotion code entered by the customer before final review. Public means transferable: a campaign audience is not a coupon access restriction. The owner sets active dates, minimum basket amount, percentage and maximum discount, plus a total redemption limit; one code per order, no stacking or per-person guarantees. Server quote calculation checks the rules and current restaurant catalog. Checkout rechecks them and consumes a redemption atomically with the order, preserving the code, rule version and discount in the immutable snapshot. Concurrent checkout at the final redemption limit permits only one consumption; idempotent replay never consumes again. An expired/exhausted or changed offer returns a new quote for explicit review, never silently charges the undiscounted amount. Rejected/cancelled orders retain their redemption allocation initially; the UI explains that they do not automatically restore the offer limit. Report code use at submission separately from completed, paid orders and corrected payments; neither code use nor recorded payment proves campaign-caused revenue. Recipient-exclusive offers and verified per-person limits need a later identity design.

Use the database for durable campaign work; select a minimal worker compatible with the existing deployment rather than a speculative orchestration framework. Enforce tenant spending/send caps and an owner pause switch. No live marketing until sender onboarding, provider requirements and applicable consent rules are verified for the pilot jurisdiction. Provider choice and pricing are open, not implied capabilities. The public demo previews synthetic campaigns with real sending disabled server-side.

## Data and lifecycle constraints

Add restaurant membership and operational/payment/campaign records with migrations and rollback compatibility. Immutable receipts and mutable operational state have separate responsibilities. Where order submission creates the operational record, commit it atomically with the receipt, or establish an equivalent durable handoff before acceptance.

Existing anonymous-guest cleanup must not accidentally delete a live restaurant's business records or consent ledger. Before live migration, define independent retention policies for guest access, restaurant orders, payment records and marketing evidence, including deletion/export and backups. Revoked/expired guest access remains denied even when restaurant records must be retained. Exact periods depend on the pilot jurisdiction and business requirements and are a live-launch blocker until resolved.

Existing mock-v1 prices, synthetic addresses and fixed Placed semantics cannot silently become live invoicing rules. Before pilot, validate the restaurant's currency, charge/tax configuration and receipt requirements; version live pricing and preserve old mock receipts. The public demo and live pilot require isolated data and provider settings. Do not repurpose local_demo: it remains the existing limited fixture preview, not a fallback for failed live operations.
